# Product Spec: Facebook Integration Module

**Status:** Draft
**Parent product:** Two-Click Lead Machine
**Audience:** Product / stakeholder reference — no implementation detail included
**Source:** Derived from `docs/modules/fb-integration-PRD.md` and the module's shipped Phase 1 build

---

## 1. Overview

The Facebook Integration module is what turns a business owner's basic
details into a live Facebook lead ad, without them ever touching Facebook's
own ad tools directly. The owner connects their Facebook account, the
product writes the ad copy and picks an image for them, they set a daily
budget, and they click Launch. From that point on, every person who fills
out the ad's lead form on Facebook flows automatically into the product's
lead-notification system.

This module owns the entire relationship with Facebook on the business's
behalf: the connection itself, the ad campaign, and the subscription that
lets new leads arrive in real time. No other part of the product ever talks
to Facebook directly.

It ships in two stages:

- **Phase 1 (shipping now):** the connection and campaign creation are
  brokered through a third-party ad-automation partner rather than a direct
  relationship with Facebook's parent company, Meta. This lets the product
  get in front of real businesses sooner, without waiting on Meta's own
  lengthy platform-approval process.
- **Phase 2 (later, post-validation):** once the product has proven itself
  with real users, the same experience is rebuilt on a direct relationship
  with Meta, removing the third-party partner from the equation. The owner's
  experience does not change when this happens.

## 2. User Journey

1. Business owner has already entered their basic business details
   elsewhere in the product (name, industry, location, what they're
   offering).
2. Owner connects their Facebook account and selects which Facebook Page
   and ad account to run the campaign on.
3. Product automatically writes ad headline/body copy and selects a
   matching image — the owner doesn't write anything themselves.
4. Owner sets (or accepts a suggested) daily budget and a target radius
   around their business.
5. Owner clicks Launch. The ad goes live on Facebook within their own ad
   account.
6. Any consumer who fills out the ad's lead form on Facebook is
   automatically captured and handed off to the product's lead-response
   system, in real time.

## 3. Functional Requirements

### 3.1 Connecting Facebook

- **FR-1:** The owner must be able to connect their real Facebook account
  through Facebook's own login and permissions screen — this cannot be a
  simulated or internal-only flow.
- **FR-2:** After connecting, the owner must be able to choose which
  Facebook Page and which ad account the campaign will run under.
- **FR-3:** The product must remember this connection so the owner is not
  asked to reconnect for every new campaign.

### 3.2 Ad Creation

- **FR-4:** The product must automatically generate ad headline and body
  copy based on the business's name, industry, location, and stated offer
  — the owner does not write ad copy themselves.
- **FR-5:** The product must automatically select a matching image for the
  ad.
- **FR-6:** Ad copy and image generation must reliably produce at least one
  usable result per business without the owner needing to intervene or
  retry.
- **FR-7:** The generated ad is independent of any other content the
  business has created elsewhere in the product (e.g. their giveaway
  webpage) — it is not required to match or reuse that content.

### 3.3 Budget & Targeting

- **FR-8:** The owner must be able to set a daily ad budget.
- **FR-9:** The owner must be able to set (or accept a default) geographic
  targeting radius, defaulted from the business's own location.

### 3.4 Launch

- **FR-10:** The owner must be able to launch the campaign with a single
  action once creative, budget, and targeting are set.
- **FR-11:** Once launched, the campaign must appear as a live, running
  campaign inside the owner's own Facebook Ads account — not just inside
  this product.
- **FR-12:** The ad must use Facebook's built-in native lead form, which
  automatically pre-fills a consumer's name, phone, and email from their
  Facebook profile when they respond to the ad.

### 3.5 Lead Delivery

- **FR-13:** The moment a consumer submits the native lead form, that lead
  must be captured and made available to the product's lead-response system
  in real time — no manual export or delay.
- **FR-14:** The product must confirm that live lead delivery is actually
  active immediately after launch, rather than simply assuming the
  connection succeeded.
- **FR-15:** This module is the only part of the product that ever holds
  the business's Facebook connection — no other module asks the business to
  connect Facebook a second time or holds its own separate credentials.

### 3.6 Billing

- **FR-16:** Ad spend is billed directly to the business's own connected
  Facebook ad account — the product does not collect, hold, or mark up ad
  budget on the business's behalf.

## 4. Non-Functional Requirements

- **NFR-1 (Reliability):** A launched campaign's live-lead connection must
  be verified working immediately after launch, not left to fail silently;
  if verification fails, the owner must be told rather than left believing
  the campaign is fully live.
- **NFR-2 (Trust & Transparency):** The Facebook connection step must be a
  genuine, recognizable Facebook consent screen — never an experience that
  could be mistaken for collecting the owner's Facebook password directly
  within this product.
- **NFR-3 (Continuity across phases):** The owner-facing experience
  (connect → generate ad → set budget → launch) must look and behave
  identically whether the underlying connection is brokered through the
  Phase 1 partner or a direct Phase 2 relationship with Meta — the owner
  should never need to know, or care, which is in effect.
- **NFR-4 (Data ownership boundary):** Only this module may hold a
  business's Facebook/ad-platform credentials at any point in time; every
  other part of the product receives leads and spend data secondhand,
  never direct platform access.
- **NFR-5 (Compliance):** Ad copy generated for regulated industries (e.g.
  financial services, insurance) must be reviewed for required disclaimers
  before use, consistent with how the rest of the product handles regulated
  content.
- **NFR-6 (Resilience to partner dependency):** Because Phase 1 relies on
  an outside partner's own access to Facebook, an outage, policy change, or
  loss of access on the partner's side is treated as a real risk to
  campaign launches — not a hidden dependency.
- **NFR-7 (Migration continuity):** Moving a business from the Phase 1
  partner-brokered connection to the Phase 2 direct connection must not
  require the owner to rebuild their campaign from scratch, and should
  avoid requiring them to reconnect Facebook if at all possible.
- **NFR-8 (Usability):** An owner with no prior Facebook advertising
  experience must be able to complete the entire connect → launch flow
  without needing outside instructions.

## 5. Out of Scope (Non-Goals)

- Writing or generating the business's giveaway webpage content — that is
  owned by a separate part of the product.
- Capturing, notifying on, or scripting responses to leads once they
  arrive — that is owned by the lead-response system; this module's job
  ends the moment a lead is handed off.
- Holding or managing ad spend as a wallet or balance — Facebook bills the
  business directly.
- A custom landing page for the ad — the ad always points to Facebook's own
  native lead form.
- Any other part of the product independently connecting to Facebook — this
  module is the sole gatekeeper of that relationship.
- A direct (non-partner) relationship with Meta in Phase 1 — deliberately
  deferred to Phase 2, after the product is validated with real users.

## 6. Success Criteria

**Phase 1 (soft launch):**
- An owner can connect Facebook and see a genuinely live campaign appear in
  their own Facebook Ads account after clicking Launch.
- Live lead delivery is confirmed active immediately after launch, not
  assumed.
- Ad generation reliably produces a usable headline, body copy, and image
  for a business without manual rework.

**Phase 2 (owned integration):**
- The same guarantees above continue to hold, now delivered through a
  direct relationship with Meta, with existing Phase 1 businesses carried
  over without a confusing or repeated setup experience.

## 7. Open Questions (Product-Level)

- Which third-party partner should be used for Phase 1 — not yet decided;
  needs evaluation for whether it genuinely supports Facebook's native lead
  form flow, not just generic ad posting.
- What specifically triggers the move from Phase 1 to Phase 2 (a number of
  active campaigns, a revenue milestone, elapsed time)? Not yet defined.
- When moving a business from Phase 1 to Phase 2, will they need to
  reconnect Facebook, or can the existing connection be preserved? Needs to
  be understood before a Phase 1 partner is chosen, not after.
- Since ad copy is generated independently from the business's giveaway
  webpage content, should the two be checked for consistency so a consumer
  doesn't see one message in the ad and a different one on the page they
  land on afterward?

## 8. Key Risks

- **Partner dependency (Phase 1):** campaign launches depend on an outside
  partner's own standing with Facebook; if that access is revoked or
  degraded, campaigns break for reasons outside the product's control.
- **Migration risk (Phase 1 → Phase 2):** switching partners could force
  existing businesses to reconnect Facebook or re-grant permissions if not
  planned for up front.
- **Platform approval (Phase 2):** Meta's own review and verification
  process for a direct integration is the single biggest long-term
  timeline risk for this module.
- **Ad policy compliance:** Facebook can reject or disable ad accounts for
  policy violations; regulated-industry ad copy carries elevated risk here.
- **Concentrated credential risk:** because this module is the sole holder
  of each business's Facebook connection, a broken or expired connection
  simultaneously breaks both ad management and lead delivery for that
  business.
- **Message mismatch:** the ad and the webpage it eventually points a
  consumer toward are generated independently, so their messaging could
  drift apart over time.
